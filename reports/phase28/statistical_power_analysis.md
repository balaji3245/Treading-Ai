## Executive Summary

This audit assesses the statistical power, estimation uncertainty, and detection limits of the backtesting methodology deployed in Phase 27. Using exclusively the authorized 2021–2022 development partition, this analysis evaluates whether historical sample sizes ($N = 40$ to $133$ trades across two calendar years) were mathematically capable of reliably detecting the pre-registered minimum economically meaningful expectancy ($\delta = +\$0.50/\text{oz}$).

### Primary Audit Findings:
1. **Severe Underpowering Across All Phase 27 Hypotheses:** True statistical power ($1 - \beta$) to detect the target economic edge of $+\$0.50/\text{oz}$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ (two-tailed) ranged from **6.38% to 42.72%**. None of the candidate hypotheses achieved even 50% power, falling far short of the scientific benchmark of 80% power.
2. **Standard Error Dominates Expected Edge:** Due to high gold price volatility ($\sigma_{\text{trade}} = \$2.81$ to $\$9.79/\text{oz}$), standard errors of mean trade expectancy ($\text{SE} = \sigma / \sqrt{N}$) ranged from **$\$0.28$ to $\$1.44/\text{oz}$**. For four of the five strategies, the sampling noise ($\text{SE}$) was larger than the entire target effect ($\$0.50/\text{oz}$).
3. **Massive Sample Size Deficit:** To detect a true $+\$0.50/\text{oz}$ edge with 80% power, required sample sizes range from **$N = 249$ trades** (for narrow-range drift reversion) to **$N = 1,312$ – $3,012$ trades** (for volatility and range momentum). At historical trade generation rates (20 to 65 trades/year), accumulating sufficient power requires **5 to 131 years of stationary data**.
4. **Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) Inflation:** At the observed sample sizes, the minimum detectable effect with 80% power ranges from **$+\$0.79/\text{oz}$ to $+\$4.05/\text{oz}$**. A strategy would need to generate an extraordinary, unrealistic edge ($> \$2.00/\text{oz}$) to achieve statistical significance.
5. **Epistemological Fallacy #1 (Nonsignificance $\neq$ No Edge):** Failing to reject the null hypothesis ($p > 0.05$) in an underpowered experiment does **not** prove that no edge exists. It means the experiment was blind to small-to-moderate economic edges.
6. **Epistemological Fallacy #2 (Positive PnL $\neq$ Edge Exists):** In an underpowered regime, positive sample backtest PnL is frequently generated by random walk drift and data snooping across multiple rules. Positive backtest PnL without statistical significance provides zero evidence of true profitability.

## 1. Mathematical Framework for Statistical Power & MDE

For a trading strategy producing trade returns $X_i \sim \mathcal{D}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, we test the null hypothesis of zero economic edge against the alternative hypothesis of a profitable edge:
$$H_0: \mu \le 0 \quad \text{vs.} \quad H_1: \mu \ge \delta$$

Where $\delta = +\$0.50/\text{oz}$ is the pre-registered minimum economically meaningful expectancy.

### Analytical Equations:
1. **Standard Error of Mean Expectancy:**
   $$\text{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}$$

2. **Statistical Power ($1 - \beta$) at Significance Level $\alpha = 0.05$ (Two-Sided):**
   $$z_{\alpha/2} = \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha/2) \approx 1.960$$
   $$\text{Non-centrality Parameter: } \lambda = \frac{\delta - 0}{\text{SE}} = \frac{\delta \cdot \sqrt{N}}{\sigma}$$
   $$\text{Power} = 1 - \Phi(z_{\alpha/2} - \lambda) + \Phi(-z_{\alpha/2} - \lambda)$$

3. **Required Sample Size for 80% Power ($1 - \beta = 0.80$, $z_{1-\beta} = 0.8416$):**
   $$N_{\text{req}} = \left( \frac{(z_{\alpha/2} + z_{1-\beta}) \cdot \sigma}{\delta} \right)^2 \approx \left( \frac{2.8016 \cdot \sigma}{\delta} \right)^2$$

4. **Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) at 80% Power:**
   $$\text{MDE}_{80\%} = \frac{(z_{\alpha/2} + z_{1-\beta}) \cdot \sigma}{\sqrt{N}} \approx \frac{2.8016 \cdot \sigma}{\sqrt{N}}$$

5. **Effective Sample Size under Serial Dependence ($N_{\text{eff}}$):**
   $$N_{\text{eff}} = N \cdot \frac{1 - \rho_1}{1 + \rho_1}$$
   Where $\rho_1$ is the first-order serial autocorrelation of trade returns.

## 3. Epistemological Hazards in Underpowered Quant Research

### A. Why Nonsignificance Does NOT Prove "No Edge Exists"
In hypothesis testing, a $p$-value measures the probability of observing data at least as extreme as the sample under $H_0$. 
- When an experiment has **statistical power of 8% to 15%**, the probability of failing to reject a false null hypothesis (Type II error rate) is:
  $$\beta = 1 - \text{Power} = 85\% \text{ to } 92\%$$
- Under these conditions, even if a true, robust economic edge of $+\$0.50/\text{oz}$ is operating in the market, an analyst will fail to find statistical significance **9 out of 10 times**.
- Therefore, concluding that "no edge exists in gold momentum" based on $N = 46$ trades over two years is a **fallacy of underpowered inference**. The correct scientific conclusion is: **the experiment lacked the statistical resolution to detect the hypothesis**.

### B. Why Positive Backtest PnL Does NOT Prove "An Edge Exists"
Conversely, researchers frequently commit the opposite error: observing a positive sample mean ($+\$0.94/\text{oz}$ or $+\$1.41/\text{oz}$) and concluding that the strategy is profitable.
- In low-powered regimes with small $N$ and large $\sigma$, the sampling distribution of the mean has wide tails:
  $$95\% \text{ CI for } \mu = \bar{X} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}$$
- For `HYP-27-CONSECUTIVE-RANGE-MOMENTUM`, the 95% confidence interval is:
  $$[+\$1.41 - 1.96(1.44), +\$1.41 + 1.96(1.44)] = [-\$1.41/\text{oz}, +\$4.23/\text{oz}]$$
- Because the confidence interval encompasses zero, a purely random Brownian motion with zero drift ($\mu = 0$) will produce a sample mean of $+\$1.41/\text{oz}$ approximately **16.4% of the time** purely by luck.
- When researchers test multiple candidate rules (e.g. 5 hypotheses in Phase 27), the probability that at least one rule shows positive PnL by chance exceeds **$60\%$**.
- **Auditor Mandate:** A positive backtest PnL without adequate sample size and statistical significance is noise. Presenting underpowered backtest profits as evidence of commercial edge is unacceptable.

## 4. The Structural Paradox of M5 Systematic Trading

The quantitative findings reveal a fundamental structural paradox for low-frequency intraday trading on M5 XAUUSD:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       THE INTRADAY QUANTITATIVE PARADOX                         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                 |
|   Low Frequency Intraday Setups (20 - 70 trades/year)                           |
|   ├── Advantage: Avoids compounding fixed execution costs ($0.25/oz).           |
|   └── Fatal Flaw: Generates only N ≈ 40-140 trades over 2 years.                |
|                   SE ($0.50-$1.40) dwarfs target edge ($0.50).                  |
|                   Statistical power is 6% - 15%.                                |
|                   Cannot prove or disprove edge within realistic sample window. |
|                                                                                 |
|   High Frequency Intraday Setups (500 - 2,000 trades/year)                      |
|   ├── Advantage: Generates massive sample size (N > 1,000 in 1-2 years).        |
|   |               High statistical power (>80%).                                |
|   └── Fatal Flaw: Consumed entirely by the frictional cost wall.                |
|                   Round-turn cost ($0.25-$0.50/oz) eats 50%-100% of gross move. |
|                   Net expectancy collapses to negative.                         |
|                                                                                 |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Conclusion:
Intraday M5 systematic trading in spot gold occupies a "no-man's land":
- If trade frequency is low, the strategy is **statistically blind** (underpowered).
- If trade frequency is high, the strategy is **financially suffocated** (cost wall).

The only mathematical escape from this paradox is to shift the investment horizon to **multi-day swing positions** (where excursion targets of $\$20.00–\$50.00/\text{oz}$ reduce frictional drag to $< 1.5\%$ and trade holding periods span days to weeks), or to acquire **10+ years of high-resolution institutional tick data**.
